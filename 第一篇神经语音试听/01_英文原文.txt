Learning with a Purpose.

Initially, many students regard a diploma as their ultimate objective. Yet a qualification is only one component of an education. What matters more is whether learners can interpret a difficult paragraph, formulate a coherent argument, and apply an abstract concept to a realistic situation. A good instructor will therefore encourage a student to question an assumption, clarify a notion, and distinguish an apparent fact from a reasonable inference.
At our college, each participant must undertake a small research project. Students first prepare an outline, then develop a hypothesis and gather empirical evidence. They submit a draft to a tutor, who may reject a superficial explanation but will offer constructive counsel. A scholarship rewards persistence and integrity as well as achievement. The dean hopes that this systematic approach will broaden students' intellectual interests. Not only does purposeful study deepen understanding, but it also gives learners the confidence to tackle unfamiliar problems. A university should cultivate independent judgment rather than make perfection its only criterion of success.
